# Strandex — Website Build Plan

**One-page marketing site for a Singapore-based wire rope & lifting gear inspection company, serving the construction industry.**

Prepared as a build brief for Claude Code. Content in `[brackets]` is a placeholder — real information is needed before launch. Everything else is draft-ready copy: refine the wording if useful, but don't introduce facts (dates, certifications, client names, credentials) beyond what's specified here or supplied by Strandex directly.

---

## 1. Brief

- **Company:** Strandex
- **What they do:** Independent inspection of steel wire ropes and lifting gear (slings, rigging) used on construction sites — cranes, hoists, and related equipment.
- **Location:** Singapore
- **Site type:** One page, six sections, no CMS needed.
- **Primary audience:** Procurement and WSH/EHS contacts at main contractors and construction firms vetting inspection vendors.
- **Why it exists:** bizSAFE Level 3 is a near-mandatory vendor requirement for main contractors and government-linked projects in construction. It signals an audited Risk Management Plan is in place — not just safety-awareness training. This site's job is to make that credibility, and the technical rigor behind it, visible fast, and to convert interest into an enquiry.
- **Tone:** Minimalist, classy, serif-led. Quiet technical authority, closer to a specialist engineering consultancy than a general contractor — not an industrial-hazard or sales-heavy register.

---

## 2. What shaped this brief

I read all three references for structure and tone, then wrote original copy for Strandex — nothing below is copied from them.

- **Macvin:** leads with a credibility statement, breaks work into clearly named services, closes with a client logo wall and a simple CTA. Useful pattern: credibility first, services second.
- **InspecPro:** ties its authority to one specific accreditation (SAC-SINGLAS) and a named technology partnership, rather than general claims. Useful pattern: name the specific standard, don't just say "certified."
- **Vigahs:** builds trust through process — explains what inspection actually involves, in plain language. Useful pattern: technical explanation does more for credibility than adjectives.

Strandex's version leans hardest on the Macvin/InspecPro pattern, since naming the specific standard is what a bizSAFE-conscious visitor is actually scanning for.

## 4. Design Direction

### Color

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#14181C` | Primary text, dark surfaces (hero) |
| Paper | `#EDEEE9` | Primary background |
| Steel | `#8B9199` | Rules, secondary text, borders |
| Oxide | `#A63D2B` | The one accent — CTA button, certificate seal marks, section-divider ticks. Nowhere else. |

Ink is cool graphite, not a tinted black. Paper is a cool technical off-white, closer to drawing paper or mill-certificate stock than a warm cream. Oxide should read like the ink on a certification stamp — keep it rare so it stays earned rather than decorative.

### Type

Two families, clearly distinct:
- **Display — Fraunces** (headline, hero, section titles). Warm, a little unexpected, has real character at large sizes.
- **Text — Source Serif 4** (body copy, nav, form, footer). Plain and highly legible at small sizes — the workhorse.

```css
:root {
  --color-ink: #14181C;
  --color-paper: #EDEEE9;
  --color-steel: #8B9199;
  --color-oxide: #A63D2B;
  --font-display: 'Fraunces', serif;
  --font-body: 'Source Serif 4', serif;
}
```

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Fraunces:opsz,wght@9..144,400;9..144,500;9..144,600&family=Source+Serif+4:opsz,wght@8..60,400;8..60,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```

No all-caps labels, no tracked-out eyebrow text above headings — sentence case throughout.

### Layout

Left-aligned, asymmetric — not centered. Generous whitespace, body line length under 80 characters.

Between sections, a thin horizontal rule broken by small tick marks at intervals — a quiet nod to the marked intervals used when measuring rope for inspection (lay length). It's a structural device, not decoration: it appears once per section transition, consistently, and nowhere else.

### Principles
- One bold gesture — the hero photo and headline. Everything after is quiet and disciplined.
- Oxide is rare on purpose: CTA, certificate marks, divider ticks. If you reach for it a fourth place, reconsider.
- Certificates, gallery, and client logos each get a treatment suited to what they are — not one repeated rounded-card template stamped across every section.
- Copy states things plainly. Technical terms (ISO 4309, bizSAFE) are used correctly and explained in context — no unearned superlatives like "leading" or "world-class."

## 5. Technical Notes

- **Stack:** static HTML/CSS with light JS (gallery lightbox, smooth-scroll nav) is the fastest path to something classy and fast-loading — no CMS needed for six fixed sections. Rails is a fine alternative if you'd rather keep it inside your existing stack; it's just more than this page structurally needs.
- **Form handling:** no backend is planned, so use a form endpoint service (Formspree or Web3Forms both work with a static site) unless you'd rather stand up a small Rails/Sinatra endpoint yourself.
- **Images:** serve the hero photo and gallery images as WebP with a fallback; lazy-load everything below the fold.
- **Accessibility:** visible focus states throughout, alt text on every photo (especially certificate images — a screen-reader user should still get the credibility info), and confirm the oxide-on-paper and ink-on-paper contrast ratios before finalizing.
- **Responsive:** mobile-first — a WSH manager or procurement contact may well open this on a phone.
